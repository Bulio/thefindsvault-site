The Lifetime List: Glass bakeware. Which dishes are still borosilicate, and how to check the one you own

Companion page to the Pyrex episode. American Pyrex has been tempered soda-lime glass for decades, by Corning's own decision and by its own admission. Borosilicate survives a temperature swing of about 330 F; soda-lime about 99 F. Here is how to read the bottom of the dish you already own, and the makers that still state borosilicate in writing. Prices change; the sources do not.

Read the label: manufacturer codes

First 3 digitsBuilt by
PYREX in capitals, inside a circle, with 'CG'Corning Glass Works, 1940s-50s: borosilicate. Keep it.
PYREX in capitals + 'MADE IN U.S.A.'Mid-1950s Corning. Circular logo gave way to a straight line in the 1960s.
pyrex in lowercaseSoda-lime glass. Works fine, but never hot oven to cold or wet surface.
PYREX in capitals + 'Made in France'International Cookware, Chateauroux: borosilicate.
Anchor HockingTempered soda-lime by the company's own statement, not borosilicate.
