The Lifetime List: Glass bakeware. Which dishes are still borosilicate, and how to check the one you own

Companion page to the Pyrex episode. American Pyrex has been tempered soda-lime glass for decades, by Corning's own decision and by its own admission. Borosilicate survives a temperature swing of about 330 F; soda-lime about 99 F. Here is how to read the bottom of the dish you already own, and the makers that still state borosilicate in writing. Prices change; the sources do not.

The Lifetime List · glass-bakeware

  • OXO Good Grips glass bakeware — OXO's own product page says thermal-shock-resistant borosilicate, freezer to oven without thawing. The 3.0 qt baking dish with lid is listed at $26.99 on oxo.com; the range runs roughly $15-$70 by size. See it on Amazon
  • Simax borosilicate baking dish — Kavalierglass, Czech Republic, at a glassworks founded in 1837. States Type 3.3 borosilicate to ISO 3585. Sold in the US through Amazon, Walmart and Target, roughly $25-$41. See it on Amazon
  • Borosil glass bakeware — Mumbai, with a US arm, naming borosilicate directly in its product lines. Roughly $10-$25 a dish. See it on Amazon
  • European PYREX (all caps, Made in France) — The capital-letter PYREX is licensed to International Cookware and made in Chateauroux, France, from tempered borosilicate. It reaches the US through third-party importers, not through the American brand. Check the bottom for all-caps PYREX inside a border and 'Made in France'. See it on Amazon
  • Marinex Forno (Brazil) — Nadir Figueiredo describes Marinex Forno as the only borosilicate ovenware line on the Brazilian market. No official US storefront: it reaches the US only through importers and third-party listings. See it on Amazon

Read the label: manufacturer codes

First 3 digitsBuilt by
PYREX in capitals, inside a circle, with 'CG'Corning Glass Works, 1940s-50s: borosilicate. Keep it.
PYREX in capitals + 'MADE IN U.S.A.'Mid-1950s Corning. Circular logo gave way to a straight line in the 1960s.
pyrex in lowercaseSoda-lime glass. Works fine, but never hot oven to cold or wet surface.
PYREX in capitals + 'Made in France'International Cookware, Chateauroux: borosilicate.
Anchor HockingTempered soda-lime by the company's own statement, not borosilicate.
