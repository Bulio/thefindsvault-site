The Lifetime List: Speed Queen. Who builds it, what is inside, how to buy one

Companion page to the episode on Speed Queen: the Ripon, Wisconsin washer with five years of parts-and-labor warranty, the Department of Energy cycle, and the three answers from the verdict. Prices change; the sources do not.

The Lifetime List · speed-queen

  • Speed Queen TC5 (top load, agitator) — Designed and built in Ripon, Wisconsin, by Alliance Laundry. All-metal transmission and five years of parts and in-home labor on the whole machine. About $1,500. Skip the Normal Eco cycle: use Heavy Duty or Permanent Press. See it on Amazon
  • Speed Queen TR7 (top load, seven-year warranty) — Seven years of parts and labor, the longest in the lineup. Consumer Reports scored its washing performance 31 out of 100, so buy it for the warranty and the build, not for the cleanest wash. See it on Amazon
  • Before you buy: find the technician — Speed Queen does not run its own repair fleet. Call a local repair shop and ask whether they service Speed Queen before you pay: the warranty is only as good as the person who drives to your house.
