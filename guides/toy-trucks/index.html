Tonka today: which truck still has steel in it

As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases. The product links on this page are affiliate links.

Two Tonka lines sit side by side under the same yellow paint. One still has a real steel dump bed; the other is plastic and zinc alloy. This page tells them apart, and shows how collectors date an old steel Tonka by its logo.

If you want the steel

The Steel Classics line still has a real steel dump bed on a partly plastic body. It is the one that keeps the thing that made the name. Check the listing says steel.

  • Tonka Steel Classics Mighty Dump Truck — Real steel dump bed, sturdy plastic body. The yellow one most people picture. See it on Amazon →

If plastic is fine

The Next Gen line is plastic and zinc alloy, with no steel at all. Cheaper, lighter, a different toy.

  • Tonka Next Gen Mighty Dump Truck — Plastic and zinc alloy, no steel. Sold at major toy retailers.

The one you already own

A genuine steel Tonka from the sixties or seventies, used, is still one of the toughest toys ever sold.

  • Keep the old steel one — Read the logo to date it. Condition and the original box are most of the value.
