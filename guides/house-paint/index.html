House paint: how to read the data sheet before you pay

As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases. The product links on this page are affiliate links.

Premium interior paints from three different companies run from 40 to 46 percent volume solids on their own data sheets. That number, the binder and the MPI approval tell you more than anything printed on the front of the can. This page puts the three sheets side by side and shows where to find the one for any paint you are about to buy.

  2. Ignore these four

Read these three

On the technical data sheet: volume solids (how much stays on the wall), the binder (100% acrylic, styrene acrylic or vinyl acrylic), and the MPI number (what an independent institute tested it for).

  • Volume solids — Wet film times volume solids is what stays on the wall. At the same price, more solids means more of what you paid for.
  • The binder — The front of the can will not name it. The data sheet does, in one line.
  • The MPI number — The only number on the sheet the manufacturer does not grade by itself. Approval is for particular uses, not every surface.

Ignore these four

Paint and primer in one works, but almost every can says it. One-coat guarantees exclude colors and untinted bases. Lifetime warranties replace the paint or refund it, never the labor. The name tells you who sells it, not what is inside.

  • Keep the receipt — Both lifetime warranties we read ask for the original sales receipt. Photograph it and keep it with the house papers.
