Read these three

On the technical data sheet: volume solids (how much stays on the wall), the binder (100% acrylic, styrene acrylic or vinyl acrylic), and the MPI number (what an independent institute tested it for).

Volume solids

Volume solids

Wet film times volume solids is what stays on the wall. At the same price, more solids means more of what you paid for.

Ignore these four

Paint and primer in one works, but almost every can says it. One-coat guarantees exclude colors and untinted bases. Lifetime warranties replace the paint or refund it, never the labor. The name tells you who sells it, not what is inside.

Keep the receipt

Both lifetime warranties we read ask for the original sales receipt. Photograph it and keep it with the house papers.

Check it yourself

The data sheet promises a dry film thickness at a given wet film. This is the tool that tells you, while you are painting, whether you are actually laying that much on the wall.

The brush outlives the paint

Volume solids only end up on the wall if something lays them there evenly. A trade brush costs more than a supermarket one, keeps its shape for years of washing out, and is the cheapest way to actually get the wet film the data sheet assumes.
