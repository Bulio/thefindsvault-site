Model kits: how to tell a decades-old mold from a new tool before you buy

Plastic model kits are reissued for decades from the same steel molds, often in new boxes with new artwork. The five checks on this page date the plastic, not the printing, and help you choose between a nostalgic reissue and a modern new-tool kit.

Before you pay: five checks

In the order we would do them in the store or on a listing page. The second one settles almost every doubt in under a minute.

  • 1. Do not trust the copyright year on the box — It dates the printing, not the tooling. A 2023 box can hold parts from a mold engineered in the mid-1970s.
  • 2. Look the product number up on Scalemates — The free kit database lists every release of the same plastic. Scroll to the oldest entry and read its tag: New tool marks the year the steel was cut; Rebox and New decals mean the same parts again.
  • 3. Raised or recessed panel lines — Raised ridges usually point to tooling from the seventies or eighties; fine engraved lines are the norm on modern molds.
  • 4. Look for the halo on the parts — Thin excess plastic along the edges (flash) is the mark of a worn tool. It cleans up, but plenty of it means more work.
  • 5. Read the first digits of the product number — Revell new-tool kits often carry numbers starting 03 or 04 with modern digital art. Treat it as a hint and confirm with check 2.

Old tool or new tool: which one to buy

Neither is wrong. They are different products that often sit on the same shelf at the same price, so decide which one you want before you look at the box art.

  • Old-tool reissue — The shape you built as a kid, usually cheaper, with raised detail and more filling and sanding. Good for nostalgia and for a child's first build.
  • New tool — Designed with current methods: recessed detail and closer fit. Look it up first; the box art alone will not tell you.
  • Buying online — Copy the product number from the listing and check its history before comparing prices: listings usually show only the repainted box art.
